# LifeOS — Product Vision

## Purpose

LifeOS helps people who feel uncertain about their direction in life discover what matters to them and take practical steps toward a more meaningful, self-directed everyday life.

## The problem

Periods of transition — changing careers, losing structure, ending a relationship, entering a new life stage, or simply feeling stuck — can leave people with many possibilities but little clarity. Traditional task managers assume users already know what they want to accomplish; generic chatbots often lack a consistent process connecting reflection to action.

## Intended audience

Adults seeking non-clinical personal growth and structure, especially during life transitions. LifeOS is intended for voluntary self-reflection, goal-setting, planning, and review, not therapy or diagnosis.

## Product promise

**From self-understanding to meaningful action.**

LifeOS should help a user:
1. Reflect on what matters and what currently feels out of balance.
2. Choose a self-defined priority without being judged or pushed.
3. Translate that priority into a realistic, achievable next step.
4. Reflect on what happened and adapt over time.

## Product pillars

### Life Compass
Explore life domains, values, sources of energy, and areas where the user wants change.

### Life Strategy
Turn a personally meaningful direction into goals, trade-offs, and small actions.

### Daily Companion
Offer brief, optional planning and reflection experiences that respect time and energy.

### Personal Memory
Maintain context with explicit user visibility, correction, export, and deletion controls.

### Life Review
Help the user recognize patterns, revisit priorities, and acknowledge progress without reducing life to a score.

## Design principles

- **User autonomy first:** The user sets priorities; AI offers options, not directives.
- **Small, practical steps:** Reduce overwhelm; focus on actions that fit real life.
- **Nonjudgmental tone:** Avoid shame, manipulation, or pressure to be productive.
- **Human connection matters:** Encourage appropriate offline relationships and support.
- **Privacy by design:** Data minimization, informed consent, and secure handling of personal information.
- **Transparent AI:** Be clear about limitations, uncertainty, and what information the assistant retains.
- **Safety boundaries:** Do not diagnose, deliver therapy, or claim to replace clinicians; direct users to appropriate human help for acute safety concerns.
- **Accessibility:** Use simple language and inclusive, accessible interfaces.

## Success criteria

Early product success means a user can describe a self-chosen priority, identify a realistic next action, and feel more able to act — not that they spend more time in the app. Validate these assumptions through voluntary user feedback before making effectiveness claims.

## Non-goals

- Providing medical or mental-health treatment.
- Making major life decisions on users' behalf.
- Ranking people by happiness, worth, or performance.
- Monetizing private reflections through advertising or sale of sensitive data.

## Long-term opportunity

A trustworthy personal guidance system that bridges reflection, planning, and long-term learning while keeping the individual in control.

*Status: product concept; features and outcomes are hypotheses to be validated.*
